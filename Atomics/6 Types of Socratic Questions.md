# 6 Types of Socratic Questions

- **Seeking Clarification:** Exposes vague or ambiguous claims.
    
    - _Example:_ _"What exactly do we mean by 'growth'—revenue, profit margin, or market share?"_
        
- **Probing Assumptions:** Uncovers hidden premises embedded in a proposal.
    
    - _Example:_ _"What are we assuming about customer price sensitivity when projecting a 20% retention lift?"_
        
- **Probing Evidence:** Demands objective data behind claims.
    
    - _Example:_ _"What empirical data supports the claim that our pricing is uncompetitive?"_
        
- **Exploring Implications & Consequences:** Traces downstream, second-order effects.
    
    - _Example:_ _"If we enter the Indian market today, how does that impact our working capital and supply chain over 18 months?"_
        
- **Questioning Perspectives:** Evaluates alternative viewpoints and stakeholder angles.
    
    - _Example:_ _"How will our primary competitor respond to this price cut, and how do customers frame this problem?"_
        
- **Questioning the Question:** Challenges the fundamental framing of the problem.
    
    - _Example:_ _"Why are we focusing on cost reduction? Should we instead be asking how to drive value creation?"_
