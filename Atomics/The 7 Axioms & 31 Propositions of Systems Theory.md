# The 7 Axioms & 31 Propositions of Systems Theory

| **Axiom**          | **Core Definition**                                                               | **Key Propositions & Meaning**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | **Business Application / Illustration**                                                                                                 |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Centrality**  | Systems are centrally defined by emergence, hierarchy, and communication/control. | • **Communication:** Info movement enables coordination.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Control:** Decision rules redirect behavior.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Emergence:** Whole properties $\neq$ sum of parts.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Hierarchy:** Higher levels constrain lower levels. | **Toyota Quality:** Plant quality is an emergent property of _Kaizen_ routines and feedback loops, not merely individual worker skills. |
| **2. Contextual**  | System meaning depends entirely on its surrounding environment.                   | • **Holism:** Study systems as whole entities.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Complementarity:** Multiple lenses needed.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Darkness:** No single observer sees all.                                                                                               | **TCS Delivery Model:** Defined by client demand, immigration policies, cloud shifts, and forex changes.                                |
| **3. Design**      | System design involves purposeful resource/relationship imbalance.                | • **Min. Critical Specification:** Specify only essential guardrails.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Pareto (80/20):** Few causes drive most outputs.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Requisite Parsimony:** Simple as possible.                                                                | **Agile Development:** Using lightweight guardrails and product goals rather than oppressive bureaucracy.                               |
| **4. Goal**        | Systems pursue targets via purposeful pathways.                                   | • **Equifinality:** Multiple paths to 1 end-state.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Multifinality:** Same start $\rightarrow$ different ends.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Satisficing:** "Good enough" under bounded rationality.                                                             | **Startup Trajectories:** Two VCs funding identical startups with the same capital yields divergent market results.                     |
| **5. Operational** | Systems must be understood _in situ_ while operating.                             | • **Homeostasis:** Maintaining key variables.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Dynamic Equilibrium:** Active adjustment.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Suboptimization:** Local gain = global loss.                                                                                             | **Ride-Sharing (Uber):** Dynamically adjusting surge pricing and commissions to balance driver supply and rider demand.                 |
| **6. Viability**   | Controlling critical parameters ensures continued survival.                       | • **Circular Causality:** Cause-effect loops.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Requisite Variety:** Controller internal variety $\ge$ Environmental disturbance variety.                                                                                                               | **Cybersecurity:** Security infrastructure needs tool/skill variety matching the full spectrum of external threat vectors.              |
| **7. Information** | Systems create, process, and transfer information.                                | • **Info Redundancy:** Overlapping access.<br><br>  <br><br>• **Redundancy of Command:** Authority shifts to where local information resides.                                                                                                                              | **Airline Disruption:** Empowering frontline gate agents to rebook stranded passengers instantly without HQ approval.                   |
