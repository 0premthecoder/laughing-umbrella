# Detailed table on 12 Leverage Points

| **Rank** | **Leverage point**         | **Meaning**                           | **Business example**               | **Managerial implication**              |
| -------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| 12       | Constants, parameters      | Change numerical settings             | Adjusting discounts or targets     | Tunes system, doesn't change structure  |
| 11       | Buffer sizes               | Change capacity to absorb variability | Increasing safety stock            | Improves resilience, may hide problems  |
| 10       | Stock/flow structure       | Redesign physical network             | Multi-regional sourcing            | Expensive, reshapes possibilities       |
| 9        | Delay lengths              | Manage time lags                      | Faster complaint resolution        | Delays cause overshoot and oscillation  |
| 8        | Balancing feedback loops   | Strengthen corrective mechanisms      | Inventory replenishment triggers   | Supports control and stability          |
| 7        | Reinforcing feedback loops | Amplify or dampen cycles              | Platform network effects           | Drives growth, decline, or lock-in      |
| 6        | Information flows          | Change info routing and timing        | Real-time wait time displays       | Changes behavior without new rules      |
| 5        | System rules               | Change incentives and constraints     | Shift to lifetime value metrics    | Defines rewards and limits              |
| 4        | Self-organization          | Build capacity for innovation         | Discretionary time for exploration | Enables system evolution                |
| 3        | System goals               | Change system purpose                 | Shift from sales to well-being     | Reorganizes metrics and allocation      |
| 2        | Mindset/paradigm           | Change underlying assumptions         | Shift to circular economy          | Defines what is considered rational     |
| 1        | Transcending paradigms     | Remain open to reframing              | Designing ambidextrous models      | Deepest leverage: questioning the frame |
