# Properties of System

1. **Emergence:** The system as a whole exhibits properties that none of its constituent parts possess in isolation (e.g., water's "wetness" is not found in hydrogen or oxygen; a company's "innovation" cannot be isolated to just the R&D department).
    
2. **Non-Linearity:** Cause and effect are disproportionate; small interventions can produce massive, catastrophic, or exponential outcomes (e.g., the Volkswagen "Dieselgate" algorithm triggering a massive global reputational collapse).
    
3. **Feedback Loops:** Information about a system's output is routed back as input, either amplifying (reinforcing) or dampening (balancing) future behavior (e.g., Netflix's recommendation engine learning from user watch habits).
    
4. **Delay:** Causes and their effects are often separated in time and space (e.g., Boeing's 737 MAX crisis was rooted in cost-cutting engineering culture changes instituted a decade prior).
    
5. **Resilience:** The capacity to recover structural integrity and function after severe internal or external disturbances (e.g., Apple sustaining innovation post-Steve Jobs).
    
6. **Hierarchy (Nested Systems):** Systems exist within larger systems, each level possessing distinct properties (e.g., Undergrad Program $\rightarrow$ Business School $\rightarrow$ University).
    
7. **Self-Organization:** The ability of a system to spontaneously reorganize itself in response to external or internal shocks without centralized top-down command (e.g., Wikipedia's crowd-sourced editorial structure).
